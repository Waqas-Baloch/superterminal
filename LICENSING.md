# Licensing, in plain English

Super Terminal is **source-available** under the
[Functional Source License, Version 1.1, MIT Future License](LICENSE)
(`FSL-1.1-MIT`). The full text is the agreement; this page is a plain summary
and is not a substitute for it.

## What you can do

- **Use it at work, on anything.** Commercial projects, client projects,
  proprietary code. No restriction, no fee, no licence to buy.
- **Read every line.** The source is public, so the privacy claims in the
  README are checkable rather than something to take on faith.
- **Change it.** Fork it, patch it, build on it for your own use.
- **Use it in your consulting work** for clients who are themselves using
  Super Terminal under this licence.
- **Teach and research with it**, non-commercially.

## The one thing you can't do

You can't take Super Terminal and sell it as a competing product or service —
something that substitutes for it or offers substantially the same
functionality. That's the "Competing Use" clause, and it's the whole point of
the licence.

## It becomes MIT after two years

Every version automatically converts to the **MIT licence two years after it
was first made available**. So this isn't a one-way door: today's release is
fully open source in 2028, and the same applies to every release after it.

## Why not MIT from the start?

Versions up to and including 1.9.x *were* MIT, and stay MIT — a licence already
granted can't be withdrawn. From 2.0.0 the licence changes so that the work
can't simply be repackaged and sold back to the same developers it was built
for. Everything an actual user wants to do is still permitted.

## Questions this usually raises

**Do I need to pay to use the CLI?** No. It's free, and the individual
experience is intended to stay free.

**Can my company use it internally?** Yes, without limit.

**Can I contribute?** Yes — contributions are accepted under this same licence.

**Is this "open source"?** Not by the OSI's definition, because of the
competing-use restriction. It is *source-available*, and it becomes genuine
open source (MIT) two years after each release.
